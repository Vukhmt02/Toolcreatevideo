In October 2018, in a house on the Via del Vasuvio that was being excavated for the first time, a workman had written a line in charcoal on a wall. Charcoal fades within weeks in the open air. This had survived because it was sealed almost immediately. It was a date, the 16th day before the calendars of November, the 17th of October.
Somebody wrote that on a wall in a house that was still being renovated. And then the mountain came down on top of it. It fits everything else. The brazers found in the houses set up for heating in a city where August afternoons run to 35°. The heavier clothing on the bodies, the pomegranates and the walnuts and the chestnuts in the storooms, which are October food, the wine that had already been pressed and sealed into jars, which happens after the harvest and never before it.
A coin found in the house of the golden bracelet carries a title that the emperor Titus took up in the second half of September 79. It was in circulation before the ash fell. So the eruption almost certainly happened in late October, most likely on the 24th, 2 months after the date that every one of us was taught.
Hold on to that because it is the pattern of everything that follows. The story of Pompei is one that was written down early, repeated confidently, and left alone by everyone since. And the biggest thing left alone is the 10,000. One more thing has to go in before the people do because without it, the behavior in the rest of this film makes no sense.
Pompei in October 79 was a building site. 17 years earlier on the 5th of February in the year 62 there had been an earthquake a serious one. The philosopher Senica wrote about it because it happened in his lifetime and it frightened people and he records that Pompei was largely thrown down and that the surrounding district was damaged.
Herculanium took it badly too. livestock died in the fields and there is a detail in his account about a flock of 600 sheep found dead which he attributes to bad air coming out of the ground. Modern estimates put it somewhere around magnitude 6. There is a relief carved in stone in a house in Pompei, paid for by a banker called Lucius Chicilius Yukundis that shows the temple of Jupiter in the forum, tilting over with its columns coming apart and an equestrian statue sliding off its base and the Vuvius gate collapsing. He
commissioned it to mark his own survival or his gratitude or both. That relief is a man putting up a picture of the worst day of his life on his own wall. And here is what matters about it. 17 years later in 79, the city had not finished repairing itself. The forum, the civic heart of the town, the place with the temples and the basilica and the administrative buildings, was still under scaffolding.
The great public buildings were partly rebuilt and partly propped. The water system had been reworked. Private houses across the town show repairs in progress. New plaster half applied. Walls rebuilt in a cheaper technique than the original. Rooms in use as storage that had been dining rooms.
The house on the Via del Visuvio where the charcoal date was written on the wall was itself being renovated. That is why a workman was writing on the plaster. Two generations of Pompeans had grown up in a town that was always being fixed. And that has a consequence that runs straight through the rest of this film.
